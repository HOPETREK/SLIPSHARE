Three steps, under a minute

No forms, no typing. The camera does the reading.

  1. Snap it

    Point your phone at the paper receipt. Photos from your gallery are not accepted.

  2. We check it

    The store and total are read, checked for edits or duplicates, and matched to a listed company.

  3. You own a bit

    A fractional share of that company is added to your account. Keep it or sell it.

Try the receipt scanner

An early demo of the camera step. It opens your live camera and reads the receipt on your phone, and the photo never leaves your device. If you tap Claim, only the store, date and total are saved so the same receipt can’t be claimed twice. No rewards are paid. Accuracy varies, and this demo can’t yet tell if a receipt is fake.

    Questions
    
      
    Is Slipshare live?
    Not yet. We're collecting a waitlist and building the first version. Nothing on this page pays out today.
    
      
    Do I really get shares?
    The plan is fractional shares held through a licensed partner. We'll name the partner and explain how it works before launch.
    
      
    Which companies are included?
    We're starting with large public companies such as Apple, Nvidia, McDonald's and Samsung. The list will grow. These companies are not partners and do not endorse Slipshare.
    
      
    Who pays for the rewards?
    We'll publish exactly where the funding comes from before launch, so you never have to guess.
    
      
    Which receipts count?
    Paper receipts photographed live with your camera, from companies on our list. Screenshots and saved photos won't be accepted.
    
      
    How do you stop fake receipts?
    Live camera only, checks for edits and duplicates, and limits per account. A receipt can only be claimed once.
    
      
    Is my data safe?
    We'll only collect what's needed to verify receipts and send rewards. A full privacy policy will be published before launch.
    
      
    What are the risks?
    Shares can go down as well as up, so a reward can be worth less later. Nothing here is investment advice.
